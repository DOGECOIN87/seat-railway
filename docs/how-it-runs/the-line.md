# The five worlds

The market cap decides how long the train is — and, at the same milestones the airline used, which world the line runs through. There are five so far. Crossing into each one changes the whole world outside, and the PA says so.

| Market cap | World | On the PA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| under $1M | **In the country** | _"Back down in the country. Please hold on."_ — when you drop back into it |
| $1M | **Above the clouds** | _"We are on the viaduct. Cloud deck below us."_ |
| $10M | **Space** | _"Guard to driver: the sky has run out. Sky is black."_ |
| $50M | **The moon** | _"Ladies and gentlemen, we are now arriving at the moon."_ |
| $100M | **Mars** | _"Ladies and gentlemen, welcome to Mars. Mind the gap, and the dust."_ |

## In the country — under $1M

![The Seat Railway train running through farmland](../.gitbook/assets/levels/1-country.jpg)

The line runs through country that never repeats. Every kilometre or so it changes: **farmland** with patchwork fields, hedgerow trees and farmhouses; **forest**, dense with conifers; **mountains**, where the rock shows through and the high ground is under snow; **desert**, with stepped mesas, boulders and cactus; and **the coast**, where the land falls away to the sea on one side. Each one blends into the next. The hills are real relief, and the line curves gently through them.

Every couple of kilometres the train runs through a **Seat Railway station**: a platform under a canopy, with signs giving the market cap and the next carriage. Billboards stand beside the line every few hundred metres, carrying the holders' adverts (see [Every seat is a billboard](../the-wall/every-seat-is-a-billboard.md)).

The sky is your own: the sun where your clock puts it, and your local weather — rain, snow, fog and storms included.

## Above the clouds — $1M

![The train on a viaduct above a sea of cloud](../.gitbook/assets/levels/2-clouds.jpg)

The line climbs onto a **viaduct** over a sea of cloud. Concrete piers drop away into the cloud tops, and the sky above is a deeper blue.

## Space — $10M

![The train on a guideway through space](../.gitbook/assets/levels/3-space.jpg)

The ground falls away altogether. The train runs on a **guideway through space**, its rails glowing cyan, with the stars out and a planet far below.

## The moon — $50M

![The train crossing the cratered moon](../.gitbook/assets/levels/4-moon.jpg)

Grey regolith, craters with raised rims, boulders, and a black sky full of stars. There is no air, so there is no haze, and Earth hangs in the sky.

## Mars — $100M

![The train crossing the red plains of Mars](../.gitbook/assets/levels/5-mars.jpg)

The furthest out the line runs — so far. Red plains and boulders, mountains on the horizon, a dusty butterscotch sky, and two small moons. By $100M the train is thirteen carriages long.

## Where you are right now

* The label at the top of the view — **Outside · In the country**, for example — names the current world.
* **The line**, the map under the view, draws every world on one line: lit as far as you have come, the train where it is, the next stop ringed, and how far you are towards it. It is measured on a log scale, so going from $1M to $2M moves it as much as going from $5M to $10M. The line does not stop at Mars: it carries on, dashed, to a stop that has no name yet.
